# Coronary Atlas: judge and product direction audit

Audited 9 October 2026. This is a decision memo for the remaining Track A build period, not a predicted judging score or a claim that other entries have been inspected. The [official Devpost overview](https://multimodal-ai-hackathon-2026-7.devpost.com/), [rules](https://multimodal-ai-hackathon-2026-7.devpost.com/rules), [Track A statement](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1T9Whog4kWF3mYB7ZMGyihGWpbJBJtsfz/view), and [submission guidelines](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1HEy6MBO_BN_aID2Nik2woYLWfuawzwHN/view) are the authority for requirements. The archived Track A and submission PDF text is in `sources/track-a.txt` and `sources/submission.txt`; the local [official-source audit](hackathon_research.md) records the checked links. The [public gallery](https://multimodal-ai-hackathon-2026-7.devpost.com/submissions/search) still says it has not been published, so there is no defensible entrant census, relative rank, or win probability.

## Decision

Keep Coronary Atlas as a **reproducible educational model-error and anatomy lab** for medical/ML learners. Lead with a short challenge: inspect one saved held-out case, identify a vessel on the 3D reference heart, guess its label, then reveal its *raw outer-fold probability* and supplied outcome. Show the app's separate live-model SHAP explanation only in the live/synthetic workflow; it cannot explain that fold model. Reveal the held-out confusion counts and, separately, what happens when a source-trained model is carried to another hospital. This uses the strongest assets: four leakage-checked tasks, anatomically named controls, exact live raw-model attribution, honest weak-vessel results, and a live input-to-visualization path. It is a product hypothesis until learners/educators are observed using it.

Do not pitch this as a diagnostic, patient-specific lesion map, novel SHAP classifier, or proven better training method. The supplied data have no lesion coordinates or raw ECG/echo signals. The app colors whole **reference** vessels, and the 55-input served model has not passed an independent patient-cohort test. [HEARTio](https://heartio.ai/) already describes investigational ECG-to-CAD and vessel estimates with a colored coronary tree and substantially deeper clinical program; it is not cleared for clinical use. [BioDigital](https://www.biodigital.com/product/the-biodigital-human) offers extensive interactive anatomy and quizzes, and [CathSim](https://cathsim.com/) targets catheterization training. Public [heart-disease/SHAP](https://github.com/global-ad-snap/heart-disease-risk-analysis) and [FastAPI/SHAP](https://github.com/Emart29/heart-disease-prediction) projects make those individual technical elements familiar. Our possible edge is the *auditable connection* between exact model output, vessel anatomy, error cases, and transport failure. This is an inference about positioning, not a uniqueness claim. See the earlier [prior-art and audience audit](product_research.md).

The educational demand is plausible but not proved. A randomized coronary-angiography teaching study found better test scores after simulator training than slides in 118 students, while a smaller 39-student cardiac 3D-tutorial study found no significant difference; neither tested this product ([Fischer et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30249587/), [McKinley et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36938223/)). A 63-clinician study in another clinical domain found a clinician-friendly explanation more acceptable than a SHAP display alone; it supports testing plain-language interpretation here, not a claim of clinical effectiveness ([study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41006498/)).

## What a skeptical Track A judge can see now

The official rubric is **predictive performance 30%, 3D visualization 25%, clinical interpretability 20%, integration 15%, implementation 10%** ([Track A statement](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1T9Whog4kWF3mYB7ZMGyihGWpbJBJtsfz/view)). These are weights, not scores assigned to Coronary Atlas.

| Rubric area | Evidence already present | Largest credible gap and next proof |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction, 30% | Nested held-out original-procedure AUC: CAD .920, LAD .831, LCX .741, RCA .717 on 303 selected patients. At 0.5, LCX/RCA recall is about .40. Training-only preprocessing, label exclusion, row-level evidence and uncertainty are recorded in the [technical report](technical_report.md) and [evaluation artifacts](../ml/metrics/evaluation.json). | The full 55-input model has no matched independent external test. Low vessel recall is consequential. Show false negatives and calibration beside the attractive CAD AUC. The AngioCAD 13-input transport study evaluates a **separate model**; see its [frozen protocol](angiocad_external_protocol.md) and [saved results](../output/research/angiocad_v1/external_results.json). |
| 3D, 25% | Licensed reference meshes, verified LAD/LCX/RCA mapping, vessel selection, rotation, zoom, tissue modes, mobile browser checks ([verification](verification.md)). | No patient-specific anatomy, lesion site or expert anatomical sign-off. Have a qualified reviewer confirm the three target mappings and the wording of the color legend before stronger anatomy claims. A polished camera move adds little if it implies lesion localization. |
| Interpretability, 20% | Exact raw-model SHAP reconstruction and measurements, visible limitations and raw-to-projected probability delta ([technical report](technical_report.md), [coherence result](coherence_projection_results.md)). | A user may read a probability as percent narrowing or SHAP as cause. Test whether learners can state the target, reference anatomy, and what each contribution means. Never present SHAP as an explanation of the joint projection. |
| Integration, 15% | Live API-to-React-to-3D flow, stale-result clearing, CSV/manual/sample input and local browser evidence ([verification](verification.md)). | Final public experience is untested; no hosted URL is required in the reviewed Track A document, but the judge needs a reliable demo video and runnable public source. Rehearse the final exact build and verify its output. |
| Implementation, 10% | Reproducible training, integrity hashes, pinned dependencies, tests, licensed geometry and setup instructions ([README](../README.md), [verification](verification.md)). | No public repository or uploaded video yet. The [submission draft](submission_draft.md) remains unpublished. The final repo must be tested from its public URL and the demo must match that release. |

The internal CAD–vessel projection lowered the mean four-target Brier by only .001198 across two repeated views of the **same** 303 patients; it made the default display coherent but did not establish external accuracy ([result](coherence_projection_results.md)). The earlier four-input CONFIRM transport experiment gave CAD AUC .656 and Brier .409 with marked overprediction; the later CONFIRM development numbers are from a previously inspected cohort and a different model ([external result](confirm_external_results.md), [development result](confirm_development_results.md)). A judge should be able to follow those distinctions in under a minute. Model sweeps, TabICLv2 and the optional LCX model remain exploratory; post-search fold results cannot be presented as a fresh independent win ([search](broad_search_results.md), [TabICL](tabicl_results.md)).

The [saved AngioCAD run](../output/research/angiocad_v1/external_results.json) reports a newly sourced hospital cohort using **13 shared inputs** and four source-only logistic models, frozen before external scoring. For each endpoint, ambiguous 26–50% bins were excluded under the [protocol](angiocad_external_protocol.md): CAD **n=345, AUC .781, Brier .144**; LAD **n=322, .723/.196**; LCX **n=331, .709/.191**; RCA **n=326, .687/.234**. At the fixed 0.5 cutoff, LCX sensitivity is **.400** (54/90 positives missed) and RCA **.316** (106/155 missed). The reduced 13-input source model's out-of-fold CAD AUC was **.790** on the original 303; the full app's .920 CAD AUC belongs to another 55-input procedure and is not an appropriate before/after comparison. On external CAD rows, observed prevalence was **80.0%** versus mean predicted probability **69.5%**. Endpoint bins, a different hospital spectrum, incomplete units/codebook and anonymous-row identity limits all matter. This is a useful transport **stress test**, not validation of the served application, a screening population, or a clinical threshold. The read-only verifier reproduced all 16 external target/cohort metrics and four source OOF pairs from saved predictions; see [the result report](angiocad_external_results.md).

## Idea inventory and kill criteria

Assessment uses five questions: **whose recurring task, present workaround, costly failure, reason to switch, and proof available before 15 October**. “Fit” means relation to the official rubric. “Evidence” describes the current product-specific evidence, not literature in general. This spans prediction, anatomy, explanation, integration, teaching and clinical directions; it is a search inventory, not every possible idea.

| Direction | User pain and switching hypothesis | Track A fit / deadline fit | Evidence now; continue only if... |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Held-out vessel error challenge**: guided case, predicted label, reveal actual/held-out outcome, false-negative and confusion-count explanation | Learners may struggle to connect probability, anatomy and misses. Slides give static labels; this would let them inspect a real model mistake interactively. Frequency and switching remain unknown. | **High / high**: prediction, anatomy, interpretation, integration. | Existing held-out predictions and viewer support it. Use only properly held-out source rows and clear data-license/privacy handling; do not expose row data that make a prediction look prospective. Kill the product claim if 3–5 target learners cannot identify the vessel and explain a false negative after the lesson, or say their existing material already does this equally well. |
| **2. Transparent transport/failure lab**: source vs new-hospital aggregate error and prevalence, with exact endpoint/input differences | ML learners and educators need a concrete population-shift example instead of a headline AUC. Current workaround is papers or notebooks; switching reason is one connected, reproducible display. | **High / medium**: strong validation methodology and explanation, but endpoint mapping is difficult. | CONFIRM's negative result and independently checked AngioCAD 13-input run are saved. Label them as separate research models. The AngioCAD CAD ranker discriminates moderately, yet CAD prevalence differs from its mean prediction and vessel misses remain high. Kill any broad “generalizes” claim without endpoint, calibration and population qualifications. |
| **3. Short assessed vessel lesson**: 3D identify → interpret number → explain error → 3-question check | Learners may repeatedly confuse LAD/LCX/RCA and what a model probability means. Existing anatomy platforms have quizzes, so a switch needs measurable improvement or less setup. | **High / medium**: aligns with 3D and interpretability, requires a small human study. | Interface has a guide, not demonstrated learning. Continue if educators say they would use this in a specific class and observed learners complete the tasks. Kill a learning-improvement claim without comparison or pre/post evidence. |
| **4. Clinical and anatomical review**: source definitions, input timing, vessel mapping and legend | A reviewer may reject clinically misleading inference. A correct map and glossary reduce preventable misunderstanding. | **High / medium**: improves credibility across rubric; reviewer availability limits timing. | No independent sign-off. Seek focused review; preserve disagreements. Kill any reviewed/clinical-accuracy wording until an actual qualified reviewer checks it. |
| **5. Prespecified compatible-cohort model comparison**: baseline versus one locked challenger | Better vessel discrimination could address poor LCX/RCA recall, but repeated same-303 searches are not an independent win. | **High / low-to-medium**: 30% prediction, yet exact field/label match is rare. | Current broad search nominated candidates only after inspection. Proceed only with genuinely unused compatible patients and locked mapping/analysis. Kill promotion if external Brier or recall/false-positive tradeoff fails. |
| **6. ECG or angiography image branch** | Could make “multimodal” literal and teach signal-to-anatomy; current app uses tabular ECG/echo observations. | **Medium / low**: image data and patient-level leakage controls would require a separate model and outcome. | Free public ECG/image sets exist, but they do not directly supply this 55-field four-target task ([dataset audit](prediction_opportunities.md)). Kill a deadline pivot unless patient IDs, labels, license and a separate independent validation path are established. |
| **7. Patient-specific CT/angiography lesion overlay** | A powerful visual demo, but users would expect correct lesion location. | **Medium / very low**. | Current data contain no coordinates. Kill any such implementation without validated per-patient imaging and lesion annotations; a whole-vessel risk color must remain a reference mapping. |
| **8. Wider model sweep, foundation models, ensemble, SMOTE or threshold tuning** | Might lift same-cohort metrics but has weak switching reason for learners and high selection-bias risk. A threshold changes error tradeoffs, not AUC. | **Medium / low** by deadline. | Existing [model search](broad_search_results.md), [TabICL](tabicl_results.md) and [threshold work](model_improvement_results.md) already cover much of this. Continue only with a prespecified error-cost objective and independent test; kill the “higher accuracy” headline based on best post-search fold. |
| **9. Clinical triage, treatment advice or causal what-if** | Potentially high-stakes demand, but the present observations, timing, selected cohort and errors do not support decisions about care. | **Low / very low** for a safe, credible submission. | No prospective utility, clinician workflow or causal evidence. Kill this positioning now. |
| **10. More 3D effects, AR, extra vessels, chatbot or generic patient report** | Can raise demo appeal, but BioDigital and others already cover rich anatomy; extra features do not fix wrong interpretation or weak validation. | **Medium / medium** for UI, **low** for winning differentiation. | Current 56 meshes and guide already provide a substantial visual. Add only where a usability task fails; kill work that cannot be tied to one rubric item and an observed user error. |

**The ranking stays the same after AngioCAD.** Its newly executed stress test makes direction 2 more concrete, but direction 1 remains the simplest way to show a user's task and connect all four rubric areas. Together they tell one story: **the model, the anatomy, the mistake, and what changed across populations**. A transport panel must never place a 13-input research model beside a 55-input app result as if they were the same predictor. Report both the moderate external CAD discrimination and the low LCX/RCA sensitivity; neither becomes a 55-input accuracy gain.

## Order of work before release

1. **Completed:** independently verify the [saved AngioCAD run](../output/research/angiocad_v1/external_results.json) against its [frozen protocol](angiocad_external_protocol.md), and document cohort, endpoint ambiguity and patient-overlap limits. The [six-page report](../output/pdf/technical_report.pdf) now contains the result.
2. **Completed:** built and verified one **error challenge** from held-out outputs with a clear observed-label reveal and raw/final-model distinction. The cases intentionally include a source-cohort false negative and false positive. They provide teaching examples, not a representative performance sample or a new population.
3. Perform a short learner/educator usability check and a separate qualified anatomy/input-definition review if people are available. Record tasks, mistakes and exact corrections. No one has been contacted and no study is claimed here.
4. Finalize the exact production build, documentation and disclosure. The official [submission guidelines](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1HEy6MBO_BN_aID2Nik2woYLWfuawzwHN/view) require a public GitHub repo with README, a 3–10 minute YouTube demo with English audio or subtitles, a Devpost description, and real-name team accounts. The [rules](https://multimodal-ai-hackathon-2026-7.devpost.com/rules) require AI-tool disclosure and one team/one track. The deadline is **15 October 2026, 00:15 IST**. The local [release ledger](next_steps_closeout.md) records that publication, video, roster and final submission remain undone; the existing [demo script](demo_script.md) is preparation, not a video.

**Release claim to defend:** “A working, reproducible research and teaching prototype that links four coronary classification outputs to labeled reference anatomy, exposes exactly what its models explain, and shows where they fail.” Any stronger statement needs a direct new test.
